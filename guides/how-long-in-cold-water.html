How long to stay in cold water?

By Łukasz Pszenny, an ice swimmer from Bydgoszcz, Poland · Updated 26 Nov 2026

There is no time you “have to” last in cold water. Still, most people want a rough idea before their first dips. Here is the rule of thumb many clubs use, and what it does not tell you.

The popular club rule: about one minute per °C

Many ice swimming groups use a simple rule: stay roughly as many minutes as the water has degrees Celsius. In 4 °C water that means around 4 minutes; in 1 °C water, around 1 minute. Experienced swimmers often cap it at a few minutes even when the water is warmer, and beginners stay well below it.

WaterRule of thumbFirst dips
0–2 °Cabout 30 s – 2 min30–60 s
3–5 °Cabout 3–5 min1–2 min
6–10 °Ca few minutes2–3 min

This is a hint, not a limit. The rule says nothing about whether a dip is safe for you today. Wind, air temperature, how tired or hungry you are, alcohol, illness, medication and your experience all change how your body copes.

Get out early when your body says so

  • strong or growing shivering, numb hands you cannot use,
  • pain, dizziness, confusion or trouble speaking,
  • simply feeling that it is enough.

Shorter is never “failing”. Your last time is only a reference point, not a record to beat.

Count the time after you get out too

Your body keeps cooling for a while after the dip (this is called afterdrop). A long dip means a longer, harder warm-up. Plan the dip so you still have the strength to dry off and dress quickly.

How Cold Dip helps

Cold Dip on Garmin watches times each stage: on shore, in water and after exit. The time in water simply keeps counting — the app never tells you to get out and never calls a time safe. With Pro you can turn on a time hint based on the water temperature you enter, using the same club rule (from 0:30 to 5:00), shown as a hint only.

These are general tips from an ice swimmer, not medical advice. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, are pregnant or unsure, talk to a doctor before you start. Never dip alone.
