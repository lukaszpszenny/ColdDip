How to start ice swimming safely

By Łukasz Pszenny, an ice swimmer from Bydgoszcz, Poland · Updated 26 Nov 2026

Ice swimming (in Poland we call it morsowanie) can become a lovely habit. The first weeks are about getting used to the cold calmly, not about toughness.

1. Talk to a doctor if you are unsure

Cold water puts real strain on the heart and blood pressure. If you have a heart or circulation condition, high blood pressure, asthma, are pregnant or simply unsure, ask a doctor before your first dip.

2. Never go alone

Join a local club or go with friends who already dip. Someone on shore should always be able to see you and help. Clubs also know safe entry points and local conditions.

3. Start in autumn and build up gradually

Starting while the water is still cooling down lets your body get used to it step by step. Some people also start with short cold showers at home.

4. What to bring

  • a towel or changing robe, warm layers, a hat, gloves and warm shoes,
  • water shoes or neoprene socks and gloves for icy shores,
  • a warm drink in a thermos and a snack,
  • a bag to keep your clothes dry and ready in order.

5. The dip itself

  1. Warm up gently on shore — a few minutes of movement, not a workout.
  2. Go in slowly and keep breathing calmly. The first cold shock makes you gasp; let it pass before you go deeper.
  3. Many beginners keep the head and hands above water.
  4. Keep the first dips short: 30 seconds to 2 minutes is plenty. See how long to stay in cold water.
  5. Get out when you feel it is time — strong shivering, pain, numbness or confusion mean get out now.

6. After the dip

Dry off and dress quickly, upper body first, and keep warming up calmly. You may feel colder for a while — that is afterdrop. Do not drive until you feel well.

7. Skip it when in doubt

Avoid dipping after alcohol, when you are ill, exhausted or very hungry, and in dangerous conditions such as thin ice, strong current or storms.

These are general tips from an ice swimmer, not medical advice. Cold water is risky. Never dip alone.
